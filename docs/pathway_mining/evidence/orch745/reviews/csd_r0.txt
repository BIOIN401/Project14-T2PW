The polarity rule is biologically right, but two implementation choices defeat it.

(a) 'accumulated' always counts as a substrate INCREASE, even when the clause says the amount fell. Example phrasings: 'accumulated less X', 'accumulated lower levels of X', 'accumulated to reduced levels', 'accumulated decreased amounts'. In that case the product-side decrease word is refused because its participant is a substrate, and then 'accumulated' credits the same substrate going DOWN. For the most common metabolite-phenotype phrasing, 'X mutants accumulated less Y', the reading runs backwards. It credits iff Y is the SUBSTRATE (contrary evidence) and refuses iff Y is the PRODUCT (the correct evidence).
