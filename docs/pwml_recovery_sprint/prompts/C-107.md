## 1. The seven routed findings

`src/t2pw/curation/apply_audit_patch.py`. Line numbers are at the C-106 tip; verify them.

### 1a. Inhibition near-synonyms defeat the contra-cue — **HIGHEST VALUE, and it reopens F-146**

`_span_licenses_actor` refuses a catalysis window that also carries an inhibition cue. The contra
set is `_ROLE_CUE_RES["inhibition"]`:

```
inhibit|suppress|repress|downregulat|down regulat|blocks|blocked|blocking
|antagonis|inactivat|abolish|attenuat
```

REV-105 tested twelve near-synonyms and **eleven defeat it**: `blockade`, `impairment`,
`disruption`, `reduction`, `loss`, `silencing`, `sequestration`, `depletion`, `ablation`,
`interference`, `quenching`.

**`reduction of` is the worst and is a genuine contradiction inside the shipped file.** It is
listed in the **catalysis** cue set (`reduces|reducing|reduction of`) and is absent from the
inhibition set. So *"the reduction of NDM-1 activity by PSA"* reads as a **catalysis cue with no
contra**, and licenses the exact promotion C-105 exists to prevent — **one rephrase away, and the
audit stage regenerates its rationale every round.**

This is the finding that matters most. It is a **`product_contract_violation`** of the same class
as F-146, reachable by paraphrase.

**Demonstrated end-to-end by the Lead through the real predicate**, not inferred from the regex
(`evidence/orch717_c107_claims_probe.py` / `.log`):

```
_span_licenses_actor('the reduction of NDM-1 activity by PSA', 'NDM-1', 'catalysis')  -> True
_span_licenses_actor('PSA significantly inhibited NDM-1 enzyme activity', ...)        -> False
_span_licenses_actor('NDM-1 catalyses the hydrolysis of meropenem', ...)              -> True

11 of 12 near-synonyms defeat the contra-cue; only 'inhibition' itself is caught.
```

**The first line is F-146, re-admitted by rephrasing one word.** The second line is the sentence the
paper actually contains, correctly refused. The guard holds against the wording it was built from
and falls to a synonym. Note that the cue is the phrase `reduction of`, not the bare word
`reduction` — a word-level test of `reduction` alone reports no cue and will mislead you.

**The trap:** `reduction`/`reduces` is legitimately a catalysis cue for **redox chemistry**
("ferrochelatase reduces …", "NADH-dependent reduction of …"). You may not simply delete it from
catalysis. Distinguish **chemical reduction of a substrate** from **reduction of an activity, a
level or an expression**. Whatever you build, prove both: a redox catalysis span still licenses,
and an activity-reduction span does not.

### 1b. Passive-with-agent fires when the agent is not the actor

```
(?:converted|catalyzed|…|formed)\b[^.]{0,80}\bby\b
```

matches whenever the passive verb is followed by `by` within 80 characters — **regardless of who
follows `by`**. The construction is only evidence when the agent named after `by` **is the actor
under judgement**. Today *"X is converted to Y by Z"* licenses **the actor W** whenever W happens
to be named within the cue window.

### 1c. Seventeen ordinary English `-ase` words over-accept, including a plural bypass

`_NON_ENZYME_ASE_WORDS` pairs singular and plural for most entries but **not all**. The singular is
listed, the plural is not, so the plural walks straight through `[a-z]{3,}ases?`.

**MEASURED BY THE LEAD AGAINST THE SHIPPED CODE, and REV-105's count is an UNDERCOUNT.**
`evidence/orch717_c107_claims_probe.py` / `.log`:

```
stoplist entries              : 33
entries with NO plural listed : 13
    briefcase ceased chase decreased erase increased lease please
    purchase released showcase staircase vase

plurals that BYPASS the stoplist and match as an enzyme noun:
    briefcases  pleases  purchases  showcases  staircases      <- FIVE, not three
```

REV-105 named **three** (`purchases`, `showcases`, `staircases`). There are **five**: `briefcases`
and `pleases` bypass too. The other eight singular-only entries do not bypass, because their
plurals are not `-ases` forms (`chases`, `erases`, `leases`, `vases` fail the `[a-z]{3,}ases?`
shape, and the `-ed` entries have no `-ases` plural at all).

**Do not fix the five.** Fix the rule so that a singular-only entry cannot bypass at all — B7 of
REV-107 fails a diff that adds exactly the named instances. **Enumerate the 17 over-accepting words
yourself as well**; the plural bypasses are a subset, not the whole.

**Read the existing comment before you "fix" this.** The stoplist is deliberately a **closed
stoplist** rather than an attempt to enumerate enzymology, and the file states the asymmetry:

> *"an unlisted English word over-accepts a CUE, which costs little on its own because the actor
> name must independently match, whereas an unlisted ENZYME under-accepts and refuses a legitimate
> repair — the failure REV-105 measured at 12 of 29 cases."*

**That asymmetry is correct and you must preserve it.** Close the plural bypass and add the
measured words. **Do not** replace the closed stoplist with an enzyme allowlist — that inverts the
asymmetry and is how C-105 round 1 refused 12 of 29 legitimate cases.

### 1d. The transport family has no enzyme-noun rule

`_ENZYME_NOUN_RE_SRC` is appended to the **catalysis** pattern only. The transport family is a
verb/noun list (`transport|translocat|…|permeas|…`) with no `-ase` rule, so **`flippase MsbA`
refuses** — a transporter named by its enzyme-family noun, which is exactly how papers name them.
`permeas` is listed as a one-off, which is the tell that the general rule is missing.

### 1e. Role `cofactor` refuses

`_ROLE_FAMILY_BY_ROLE` has no `cofactor` entry, so `_actor_role_family` returns `"other"` and
`_span_licenses_actor` falls back to `_ANY_ROLE_CUE_RE`. The docstring says that fallback is
*"strictly more permissive than the four families"* — **and it still refuses**, because
`_ANY_ROLE_CUE_RE` is built from the four families' patterns and **no family's vocabulary contains
cofactor-predicating words at all** (`cofactor`, `requires`, `dependent on`, `in the presence of`).
There is no vocabulary for the role, so the permissive fallback has nothing to match.

**Verify this mechanism before fixing it.** It is the Lead's reading of the code, not a
measurement. If the real mechanism differs, report that — the finding is the refusal, not my
explanation of it.

**Note the interaction with `PRODUCT_CONTRACT` and F-100:** `Zn2+` as a `cofactor_as_protein` is a
**declared forbidden identifier** on `PMC13231680`. Licensing the *role* `cofactor` must not become
a route to shipping metal cofactors as proteins. Those are different gates; say in your report why
they stay different.

### 1f. `mediat` matches inside "intermediate"

`mediat` is a required catalysis cue — *"ALAS2 mediates the condensation …"* is
`_actor_named_in_span`'s own docstring example and a legitimate repair. But unanchored it also
matches inside **"intermediate"**, a word that appears constantly in pathway prose.

**Measured at 0 of 397 accepted rows relying on it alone**, so there is **no live effect today**.
It is real, it is cheap, and it is the kind of thing that starts mattering the moment the corpus
changes. Anchor it.

REV-105 also recorded that this false positive currently **cancels another one** on a 140 KB
discussion section, where `mediat` matched inside *"intermediate"* and `suppress` inside
*"suppressor mutations"* — two false positives producing the right outcome for the wrong reason.
**Fixing one of a cancelling pair changes the outcome.** If anchoring `mediat` flips that span,
that is a finding to report with the span quoted, not a reason to leave it unanchored.

### 1g. Oversized evidence spans — **NOT in this card**

**62 of 692 evidence spans exceed 5,000 characters, max 176,375.** A 176 KB "span" is not evidence;
it is a document, and an ±80-character cue window inside it is close to meaningless.

**This is upstream of C-105 and outside this card's boundary.** Do not fix it. **Register it, with
the measurement, as a candidate for its own card** and say in your report which stage produces
them. The Lead decides whether it is chartered.
