## 1. The ruling this card implements, verbatim

From `DECISIONS.md` D-062, the product owner's ruling:

> When Stage 0 reads an organism that contradicts the batch request, and the reading is *correct*:
>
> 1. The run **does not** export a strict, release-ready artifact under the requested organism. The
>    request was wrong and a release-grade artifact must not be produced from a wrong request.
> 2. The run **does not** drop the paper either. The extracted pathway is **preserved as
>    `review_required`, carrying the OBSERVED organism**, with the requested scope recorded alongside
>    it so the mismatch stays auditable.

And what it explicitly does **not** authorise:

> * **It does not weaken the Stage-0 guard.** The guard's reading is correct and stays. What changes
>   is the *disposition* of a correct reading, not the detection.
> * **It does not raise the strict PWML rate.** A `review_required` artifact is not a strict export.
> * **It does not license editing the topics file.** The pinned scopes stay as the gold set pins them,
>   and `bench_acceptance.py --verify-plan` must keep returning `OK` with all ten `[pinned_override]`.

### 2b. The untruth

`OUTCOME_SCOPE_CONFLICT` is a member of `INELIGIBLE_OUTCOMES` (`rag/eligibility.py:123-131`), whose
docstring reads:

> Outcomes that mean "this paper was screened out". These are **not** pipeline failures: nothing was
> attempted, so nothing failed.

`batch/report.py:49` imports that exact set and folds it into `_INELIGIBLE_WORDS` (`report.py:140`),
so `_norm_status` maps `scope_conflict` to `STATUS_INELIGIBLE`, whose own definition
(`report.py:76-81`) repeats the claim:

> It is NOT a failure and not even a run: nothing was attempted, so nothing failed.

For these six legs **that is false on the evidence.** Something was attempted; it succeeded; at
T-104 two of them cleared the gold's own connected-core floor with 100 % enzyme and metabolite
recall (D-062's table: PMC12657337 core 4 vs floor 3, 3/3 and 6/6; PMC12421875 core 10 vs floor 7,
8/8 and 10/10). A correct-but-mis-scoped pathway was classified as a paper nothing was ever tried on.

That is the drop **merge rule 7** exists to prevent, and it is the single thing D-062 reverses.

## 3. Required change

Attach an honest disposition to the Stage-0 conflict path. Three properties must hold at your tip,
and each needs its own test:

1. **The payload survives as `review_required`.** The run carries a first-class
   `ReleaseStatus` whose status is `REVIEW_REQUIRED` (`pipeline/release_status.py:45`), not `None`.
2. **Strict export stays forbidden.** No bare `pathway.pwml` is emitted on this path, and
   `strict_acceptance_eligible` is `False`. `review_required` must never count as strict success
   (TRAP-1 / `PRODUCT_CONTRACT` 13 / `release_status.py:835`).
3. **Both organisms survive, distinguishably.** The OBSERVED organism is what the preserved record
   carries; the REQUESTED scope is recorded alongside it and is never overwritten by the
   observation. `_reconcile_stage0_scope` already asserts the request is not rewritten
   (`driver.py:545-552`) — keep that assertion working.

### The seam, and how far the run may go

**Take the narrow reading: change the disposition of the terminal record, do not restart the run
through audit / DB mapping / freeze / export.**

Reasons, in order of authority:

* D-062 itself says *"What changes is the **disposition** of a correct reading, not the detection."*
* Driving the run onward into the exporter under a contradicted organism is a **new product
  decision** nobody has taken — what an audit and a DB mapping should do when the organism is known
  wrong is not settled anywhere, and §16 of the standing instructions says to use conservative
  existing contract behaviour when a new decision is not authorised.
* Continuing the run is the one shape that could accidentally produce a strict export, which
  D-062 forbids outright.

The extracted pathway is *already on disk* at the abort point. What was dropped is the
**classification**, and restoring the classification is the whole card.

**If measurement contradicts this reading, say so in your report before implementing** — for
instance if `REVIEW_REQUIRED` provably cannot be constructed at this seam without a stage-3 gate
record, or if `PWML_REVIEW_REQUIRED_NAME` (`driver.py:1423`) turns out to be mandatory for a
`review_required` record to be readable offline. Do not silently widen the card to make a premise
true.

### Established grain to reuse — do not invent a second mechanism

`_finalize_gate_failure` (`driver.py:1820-1885`) is the existing answer to *"this run stopped, and it
still needs a first-class release classification."* Read it in full, including its docstring on
why it **carries** a verdict rather than re-deriving one (F-055, merge rule 8). Follow that pattern.
Whatever you construct must be a real `classify_release_status(...)` product, not a hand-built dict.

`classify_release_status` is in `pipeline/release_status.py`. **You may call it. You may not modify
it** — see §4.

### The status-vocabulary question — measure the blast radius before you touch it

`scope_conflict`'s membership in `INELIGIBLE_OUTCOMES` is what makes `report.py` say "nothing was
attempted". Removing it is the obvious fix and it is **not obviously safe**: `is_ineligible()`
(`eligibility.py:161`) has other callers, and `bench/metrics.py:294` and `:341` special-case
`status == "scope_conflict"` on the scorer side.

**Enumerate every caller and every consumer first, and report the enumeration.** Then choose the
narrowest seam that makes the manifest row truthful. If the honest fix genuinely requires touching
`bench/metrics.py`, **stop and report it rather than doing it** — `bench/` is live under card C-076
in another lane this session, and I will sequence it.
