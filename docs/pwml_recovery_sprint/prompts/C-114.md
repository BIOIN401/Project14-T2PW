## Why this card is diagnostics-only, stated once so nobody re-derives it

`HANDOFF.md` § 5.2a step 1 charters *one narrow acceptance/release-policy card*. The
release-policy half of that is **blocked**, and the block is documented, not assumed:
`DECISION-PACKET-D088-RUNTIME-CAP.md` shows that all four available reaction-level
replacements for the entity-anchor cap fail — on the corpus (A), on F-168's draw-instability
(B), on D-088 clause 10 in terms (C), and on `PRODUCT_CONTRACT` § 12 (D) — and its
amendment shows the acceptance instrument cannot compensate, because Priority 5 reads the
frozen runtime status and merge rule 8 forbids the scorer repairing it.

**So the release-policy change is with the product owner and the diagnostics are not.**
D-088 clause 7 — *"preserve all current raw requested anchors and unmatched-anchor
diagnostics; reclassifying them must not erase the evidence that exposed the problem"* — is
unblocked, unambiguous, and is the thing that must exist **before** any future
reclassification, not after. Building it now is what makes the later ruling implementable
without losing the evidence.

## LEAD RULING — 2026-09-02, on the two items C-114 escalated instead of deciding

**Both escalations were correct. Neither is a defect in the implementation.**

### 1. The machine-evidence budget — REVISED, and the overrun is MY drafting error

The card declared *"G11 cleanup reports only, ≤ 8 artifacts, ≤ 40 KB total."* The implementer
committed **8 cleanup reports (27,980 B — within both limits)** and, beside them, **8 `.pin.json`
verdicts (14,738 B)**, which `TEST_MATRIX.md` § 0 rule 10 makes **mandatory** for every focused
obligation, G9 proof and baseline capture. Counted together that is 16 artifacts / 42,718 B and over
on both lines.

**The budget line was drafted without accounting for the pinned launcher.** A card cannot require
runs through `pinned_pytest.py --pin-verdict` and then budget only for the wrapper's cleanup report:
the pin verdict is a **mandatory consequence of the required test discipline**, not discretionary
evidence the implementer chose to add.

**RULING, under D-019, granted explicitly and prospectively as the card owner:** the machine-evidence
budget for C-114 is revised to **≤ 16 artifacts and ≤ 48 KB, comprising up to 8 `bounded_run`
cleanup reports and their up to 8 corresponding `.pin.json` verdicts.** The committed 16 / 42,718 B
is within it. **No other budget line moves; the hand-authored budget is unchanged and was met
(192 ≤ 200 / 0 ≤ 5, and 320 ≤ 320).**

**The implementer did exactly the right thing.** D-019 says an over-budget commit *"may only be
created after RENEWED EXPLICIT AUTHORITY granting a REVISED budget; no implementer, reviewer or
test-runner may self-authorize one, and technical success or later approval does not cure one."* It
reported the ambiguity and left the ruling to the card owner. **Had it quietly decided the pin
verdicts did not count, this would be an uncured overrun regardless of how reasonable the reasoning
was.**

**Standing correction for every future card in this sprint:** a machine-evidence budget must state
whether `.pin.json` verdicts are counted, because `TEST_MATRIX.md` § 0 rule 10 makes them compulsory
wherever the card names a focused obligation. **A budget that omits them is unsatisfiable as
written.**
