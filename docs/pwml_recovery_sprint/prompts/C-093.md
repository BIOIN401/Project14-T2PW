grew because two REFUSED legs were committed" — **no longer covers this**.

**What you owe:** attribute the new buckets and the two new identity keys to the cards that produced

baseline move.

Then re-base the assertion the way C-092 did: prefer a property over an equality where one exists,
and where a literal is genuinely load-bearing say why and give it a maintenance path.

### (b) `tests/test_compound_resolution_extraction.py::test_the_golden_covers_every_committed_leg_fixture`

Growth triggers it, but **the deliberate accounting it demands IS the property**: every committed
leg fixture must be either in `GOLDEN` or in `EXCLUDED` with a reason, and it carries its own
adjacent non-vacuity test. C-092's judgement — *"converting it to a property would delete the
gate"* — is correct and is **not to be reversed**.

**What you owe:** the content decisions. For each newly committed leg the golden does not cover,
measure its compound resolution and write **either** a golden entry **or** an exclusion with a
stated rationale. Do not add blanket exclusions. Do not widen the golden by pattern.

**If a new leg's compound resolution looks wrong rather than merely uncovered, that is a finding —
register it and exclude the leg with that finding named as the reason. Do not write a wrong
resolution into the golden to make the test green.** A golden that records a defect as expected is
worse than the red.
