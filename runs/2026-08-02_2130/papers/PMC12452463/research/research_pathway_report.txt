         of chorismate to isochorismate, catalyzed by isochorismate synthase

         biosynthesis. Isochorismate to 2,3-Dihydro-2,3-Dihydroxybenzoate (DHB)

         2,3-dihydro-2,3-dihydroxybenzoate (DHB), a key intermediate in the
         pathway. EntB also functions as a carrier protein, binding DHB for
         subsequent steps. Activation of DHB : 2,3-Dihydroxybenzoate-AMP ligase
         (EntE) activates DHB by adenylation, forming DHB-AMP. This activated
         intermediate is essential for the assembly of enterobactin. Assembly
         of Enterobactin : The final steps involve the assembly of three DHB
         molecules into the cyclic trilactone structure of enterobactin. This
         process is catalyzed by the enterobactin synthase complex, which
         includes EntD, EntE, and EntF. EntD is a phosphopantetheinyl
         transferase that modifies EntB and EntF enabling them to function as
         carrier proteins. EntF is a nonribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS)
         that facilitates the condensation and cyclization of DHB molecules to
         form enterobactin ( Figure 1 ). Regulation of enterobactin production
         The production of enterobactin is tightly regulated to ensure that
         bacteria synthesize siderophores only when iron is scarce [ 30 ]. This
         regulation is primarily mediated by the Ferric Uptake Regulator (Fur)
         protein and responsive to environmental signals, particularly iron
         availability ( Figure 2 ). Figure 2. Transcriptional regulation of the
         enterobactin ( ent operon) by the Ferric uptake regulator (Fur) in
         response to iron availability. (A) Schematic representation of the
         regulatory mechanism. Under high-iron conditions (left), iron-loaded
         Fur (Fe-Fur) dimerizes and binds to the Fur binding site in the
         promoter region of the ent operon, acting as a repressor and
         inhibiting transcription. Under low-iron conditions (right), the apo-
         form of Fur cannot bind to the DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid), resulting
         in derepression and active transcription of the ent operon for the
         biosynthesis of the siderophore enterobactin. (B) Post-transcriptional
         repression of enterobactin synthesis by the RyhB sRNA (small RNA)
         under high-iron conditions. The model depicts the dual regulatory
         circuit controlling enterobactin production. Under high iron, the Fe-
         Fur complex represses transcription of the ent operon (not shown) and
         activates transcription of the RyhB small RNA (sRNA). The synthesized
         RyhB sRNA binds with complementarity to the mRNA (messenger RNA) of
         key biosynthetic genes entC and entF via their target sequences. This
         binding leads to the inhibition of their translation and/or
         degradation of the mRNA transcripts, providing a crucial post-
         transcriptional layer of repression to prevent unnecessary siderophore
         production when iron is abundant. (C) Integration of oxygen sensing
         into the regulation of enterobactin synthesis via the Fnr (Fumarate
         and nitrate reduction regulator) and Fur regulators. The model
         illustrates how enterobactin production is coupled to oxidative stress
         and iron availability. The anaerobic regulator Fnr, activated under
         low oxygen conditions, directly represses transcription of the RyhB
         sRNA. This relief of RyhB-mediated repression allows for enterobactin
         synthesis when needed. Under high oxygen conditions, Fnr is
         inactivated, de-repressing RyhB transcription. Concurrently, high iron
         levels activate Fur, which further promotes RyhB expression and
         directly represses the ent operon. This creates a concerted repressive
         signal under high-oxygen/high-iron conditions to prevent siderophore
         production. Thus, the ent operon is only fully expressed under low-
         iron and microaerobic/anaerobic conditions. Fur protein plays a
         central role in regulating enterobactin production. Under iron-replete
         conditions, Fur binds ferrous iron (Fe 2 + ) to form a complex that
         represses the transcription of enterobactin biosynthesis genes ( entA-
         entF ) by binding to their promoter regions. However, under iron-
         limiting conditions, the Fur-Fe 2 + complex dissociates, relieving
         repression and allowing the expression of the ent operon [ 31 , 32 ].
         Iron availability is the primary environmental signal regulating
         enterobactin production, with low-iron environments, such as host
         tissues or fluids, triggering the upregulation of ent genes to enhance
         iron acquisition [ 33 ]. Other environmental factors, such as
         oxidative stress and pH, can also influence enterobactin production
         indirectly by modulating iron availability or Fur activity [ 34 ].
